# Baseline, question 4

Asked 2026-08-06, before any change to the agent. The question, verbatim:

> I keep seeing the word *asmakhta*. What is it doing?

The agent's answer, unedited:

---

*Asmakhta* (אַסְמַכְתָּא, Aramaic, from the root ס-מ-ך, "to lean, support") is doing one of two quite different jobs depending on where you're seeing it. Both grow from the same idea of *leaning on* something.

## 1. In legal hermeneutics: a verse as a peg, not a source

When the Gemara says a verse is *asmakhta be'alma* — "a mere support" — it means the law under discussion is actually rabbinic, and the biblical verse quoted alongside it is a mnemonic peg or after-the-fact anchor, not the law's real source. The formula in Chullin 64b (William Davidson Edition – Vocalized Aramaic, via Sefaria, CC-BY-NC):

> מִדְּרַבָּנַן, וּקְרָא אַסְמַכְתָּא בְּעָלְמָא
> "It is rabbinic, and the verse is a mere support."

Jastrow's dictionary glosses this sense as "Scriptural text used as a support for a rabbinical enactment... a mere support or mnemotechnical aid," citing Chullin 64b and 77a. Later thinkers argue about how seriously to take these pegs — the Kuzari 3:73 and Maharal (Be'er HaGolah, Well 1) both wrestle with whether an *asmakhta* is arbitrary decoration or a deliberate hint the Sages read out of the text.

## 2. In contract law: a promise nobody meant

Here *asmakhta* is a conditional obligation the promisor never seriously expected to have to fulfill — typically a penalty or forfeiture clause ("if I don't repay by then, you keep the whole field"). The governing maxim is **asmakhta lo kanya** — an asmakhta does not effect acquisition — because the law presumes the promisor lacked real intent (*gemirut da'at*) when he agreed. It is a live dispute: Bava Metzia 66a:12-14 (Davidson Edition) resolves a mishna by assigning it to "Rabbi Yosei, who said *asmakhta kanya*" — that such a commitment *is* binding. Jastrow points to Bava Batra 168a for both sides of the maxim, and to Sanhedrin 24b, where the concept explains why gamblers are disqualified as witnesses — a wager is an *asmakhta*, so the winner's takings are tainted. Sefer HaChinukh 343:5 gives the classic case: lending against land on condition of total forfeiture at a deadline.

## How to tell which one you're reading

- Near a verse citation, in a phrase like *kera asmakhta be'alma* or *asmakhu a-kera* → sense 1, hermeneutics.
- Near *kanya / lo kanya*, conditions, penalties, gambling, or sales law → sense 2, contracts.

Romanization: *asmakhta* (SBL general-purpose scheme; no ambiguity flags fired).
