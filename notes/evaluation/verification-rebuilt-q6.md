## Detail

**1. Bava Metzia 74a:3 and :5 — VERIFIED.** The Davidson Vocalized Aramaic (Sefaria, CC-BY-NC) reads at 74a:3: `אָמַר רַב פַּפִּי מִשְּׁמֵיהּ דְּרָבָא: הַאי סִיטוּמְתָּא – קָנְיָא` and at 74a:5: `וְהִלְכְתָא לְקַבּוֹלֵי עֲלֵיהּ ״מִי שֶׁפָּרַע״. וּבְאַתְרָא דִּנְהִיגוּ לְמִקְנֵי מַמָּשׁ – קָנוּ`. The transcript's quotation, including the ellipsis join, matches character for character; "Rav Pappi in Rava's name" and the mi she-para / custom-clause description both track the fetched text and the Koren English.

**2. Shulchan Arukh CM 201:1 (Lemberg 1898) — MISATTRIBUTED in part.** 201:1 does codify situmta exactly as described: buyer marks the merchandise (רשם הלוקח רושם על המקח), retractor takes mi she-para, and "if the custom of the country is that the marking acquires outright, the sale is acquired" (ואם מנהג המדינה הוא שיקנה הרושם קנין גמור נקנה המקח). That half is VERIFIED. But the handshake, the key-delivery, and the "everything merchants customarily close with" clause are all in **201:2**: `וכן כל דבר שנהגו התגרים לקנות בו... או על ידי שתוקע לו כפו (או במקום שנוהגים הסוחרים שמוסרים לקונה המפתח)... וכן כל כיוצא בזה`. The earlier check was right. Two refinements: the printed siman heading of 201:1 does mention the handshake (דין רושם על החבית והתוקע כפו לחבירו), which likely produced the slip; and key-delivery is a Rema gloss sourced to Hagahot Maimoniyot, not the Mechaber's own text.

**3. Tashbetz Katan 398:1 (Warsaw 1902) — VERIFIED, one caveat.** Fetched: Maharam rules that one who vows to give his son to a friend to circumcise or to be ba'al brit must keep the vow, explicitly despite ein adam makneh davar shelo ba la'olam, "because it is the custom of people to vow their sons to one another to circumcise or be ba'al brit, and they keep it" — exactly the transcript's quoted clause — with the situmta gemara (BM 74) cited as the mechanism. Caveat: the fetched Maharam text never says the son is *unborn*; that is an interpretive gloss (a fair one, given the DSBL framing, and it is how the Chatam Sofer reads the parallel Mordechai passage). Moreover, the same fetched segment carries a bracketed dissent of Rabbeinu Yechiel of Paris holding that a grant while the mother is still pregnant is worthless precisely because custom cannot reach DSBL — the flat "unborn... bound" presentation smooths over a dispute sitting inside the very segment cited.

**4. Teshuvot HaRosh 13:20 (Vilna 1881) — MISATTRIBUTED.** The opening is as claimed: the fetched 13:20 opens with Reuven suing the kahal over 1,000 zehuvim in taxes "because the kahal sold every right they had in his taxes to another kahal" (לפי שהקהל מכרו כל זכות שהיה להם אצלו במסים לקהל אחר). But the fetched responsum is a procedural tax-liability dispute; its only custom discussion concerns which witnesses sign tax documents. **No situmta, no davar shelo ba la'olam, no lease-deeds appear anywhere in 13:20.** The famous holding lives in the *next* responsum, 13:21 (the tax-farm/chakhirut case), where the Rosh concedes the lease is DSBL and not in the seller's possession, then rules: "but I see the custom of the land every day that people lease from one another with no retraction... and since they so practice, it is a kinyan without retraction, as we say in Eizehu Neshekh (74a), Rav Papi said: this situmta acquires." The Chatam Sofer's and Pitchei Teshuva's own citation "Klal 13 siman 20" evidently follows a different edition's numbering; in the Vilna 1881 / Sefaria numbering the agent fetched, 13:20 does not contain the holding attributed to it. The transcript, to its credit, flagged exactly this risk in its closing caveat (truncated display, holding not verified line-by-line) — the caveat was warranted and the doubt resolves against the citation as given.

**5. Responsa Chatam Sofer CM 66 (Pressburg 1855–64) — VERIFIED.** The fetched text (segment 20 of the responsum) contains everything claimed, in one continuous passage: Tosafot BM 66a s.v. u-Manyuma on engagement penalties (דקנס שעושי' בשידוכי' מהני כיון שנוהגי' העולם ה"ל כסיטומתא), the generalization that custom cures asmakhta (בודאי כן הוא דבאסמכתא מהני מנהג העולם), and a fortiori DSBL, citing "הרא"ש בתשוב' כלל י"ג סי' כ'" — "if the custom of the world is to convey by lease-deeds things not yet come into the world, it works by reason of situmta." One footnote: on the facts before him the Chatam Sofer denies the acquisition because no such custom existed for that contract type ("I never heard of this custom") — the transcript claims only the doctrinal generalization, which is accurate.

**6. Pitchei Teshuva on CM 201:2 (Lemberg 1898) — VERIFIED.** The fetched gloss records R. Akiva Eiger, Responsa siman 134 (סי' קל"ד), citing the Yam Shel Shlomo (Bava Kamma ch. 8 siman 60) who brings it in the name of the Mordechai "and it is in the Hagahot Mordechai, end of ch. 15 of Shabbat," that situmta acquires only a thing already in existence, since it can do no more than a formal kinyan, which itself fails for DSBL. The Pitchei Teshuva then sets the Chatam Sofer CM 66 (and Rosh 13:20 as he cites it) against this. The transcript's characterization of the dispute — substitute formality vs. independent source of obligation — is a fair rendering of the recorded positions.

**7. Jastrow and the Arukh — VERIFIED, and the Arukh needed no unverifiability escape.** Jastrow (via `meturgaman word`): "סִיטּוּמְתָּא (סְטַם) seal, signet ring... B. Mets. 74a: putting the seal (mark) on the wine bought gives possession." The Arukh is on Sefaria (Sefer HeArukh, Lublin 1883); the entry under Letter Samekh reads: `סטמתא... האי סיטומתא קניא פי' חותם שטובעין בו הסוחרים על העיסקא` — "a seal that merchants stamp on the merchandise" (with the Mussaf HeArukh gloss adding the Greek "any kind of sign, especially one made by mutual consent"). Both glosses say seal, as the transcript claims.

## Overall judgement

This is a strong transcript by primary-source standards: every quotation checked matches the fetched editions verbatim, the doctrinal chain (Gemara → Shulchan Arukh → Maharam/Tashbetz → Rosh → Chatam Sofer → R. Akiva Eiger dispute) is real, and the two errors it contains are reference-precision errors, not fabrications. The two misattributions follow a common pattern: reading a siman heading or a later authority's citation as if it located the text itself. The handshake and key-delivery sit in CM 201:2, not 201:1 (the earlier check was right), and the celebrated Rosh holding on lease-deeds and things not yet in existence sits at 13:21 of the Vilna 1881 edition the agent actually fetched, not at the 13:20 it cited — the agent inherited "13:20" from the Chatam Sofer's and Pitchei Teshuva's citation convention and then presented a fetched 13:20 whose actual content (a tax-liability procedural dispute) does not contain the holding, though its own closing caveat honestly flagged that it had not verified the holding line-by-line. The one substantive smoothing is in the Tashbetz presentation, where "unborn son" is an interpretive gloss and the fetched segment itself carries Rabbeinu Yechiel's dissent denying custom's power over the not-yet-born case; the transcript does surface that dispute, but only later and through the Pitchei Teshuva rather than from the segment it quoted.
